Strategy that dies on a shelf

By Brandon Huff & Ethan Foulkes June 2, 2026 5 min read

Leadership disappears for a two-day offsite. They come back with a beautiful deck — goals, pillars, bold arrows. It lands in a drive folder. By Q2 nobody has opened it. Next year, you dust it off, update the dates, and do it again.

That’s the most common and most expensive planning anti-pattern we see: treating strategy as an event instead of a system. The work feels productive, the deck feels like an outcome — and none of it steers anything.

The tell: strategy is an artifact, not a system

As one of us put it on a recent webinar: strategic planning and execution should be a living thing — ongoing and visible — not an exercise you do that then sits on a shelf and gets dusty, and gets picked up and dusted off and put back on the shelf. The moment your strategy is a document you revisit rather than a system you operate, it has already stopped doing its job.

What it costs you

Shelf strategy doesn’t fail loudly. It fails as a slow, familiar set of symptoms:

  • You replan the same thing two or three times a quarter, never quite sure it’s settled.
  • Two teams each assume the other owns it — or own it at different levels of detail.
  • Something’s “done” — to engineering, but not to leadership, and not to the next team downstream.
  • Ask how the plan is actually going, and the honest answer is you’d have to go find out.

If that list stings, you’re not alone — it’s so common we think of it as the universal planning problem. But it isn’t bad luck or a bad team. It’s the predictable result of strategy that can’t be seen.

Before you can control conditions, you must first recognize them. You can’t manage — or fix — what you can’t see.

The fix is visibility, not another offsite

The answer to dusty strategy isn’t a better deck or a stricter framework. It’s making strategy live and visible — a connected line from your high-level goals down through objectives, initiatives, and the actual work your teams are doing, all in one place, updated as things change. When the plan is something everyone can see and operate against, it stops being a once-a-year ritual and starts being direction.

That’s a how-to of its own, and we’ll walk through the full hierarchy separately. The mindset shift is the part that matters here: if your strategy only comes off the shelf once a year, it isn’t direction. It’s decoration.

Key takeaways

  • Treating strategy as an event — offsite, deck, drive folder — is the core anti-pattern.
  • The symptoms: constant replanning, ownership confusion, “done” meaning different things, no visibility into progress.
  • You can’t manage what you can’t see; a strategy you only revisit can’t steer.
  • The fix is a living, visible hierarchy from goals to the actual work — not a better deck.
