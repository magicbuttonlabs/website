The monday.com portfolio rollup that doesn’t scale (and what we build instead)

By Ethan Foulkes June 2, 2026 7 min read

The demo is beautiful. One board, every project rolled up clean — timelines, progress, status, all in one place. Leadership nods. You buy Work Management Enterprise. The first few projects land fine.

Then someone makes a board private. And the whole thing breaks.

We’ve watched this exact sequence on call after call. We’ve also watched companies spend weeks on the phone with monday.com support trying to untangle it. So we don’t build portfolios this way — very intentionally — even though it’s the first thing most reps and consultants reach for. Here’s what goes wrong, and what we build instead.

The setup almost everyone reaches for

monday.com’s Work Management “portfolio and project” solution: individual project boards roll up into a high-level portfolio board through a connected-boards column that points at all of them. Out-of-the-box, demos well, easy upsell to Enterprise. None of that makes it the right foundation to build on.

Where it breaks

1. One private board can break the whole view

The rollup is a single connected-boards column wired to every project board. The moment someone locks one board down — makes it private, or shareable to a guest — those connections can break, and everyone loses the high-level rollup, not just the board that changed. We’ve had clients live through this, and untangling it has taken monday.com’s own support weeks. Permissioning is the single most common way we see this architecture fall over.

2. It recalculates in your browser

The aggregate isn’t computed once and stored. It’s recalculated on the front end — your machine queries every source board and rebuilds the rolled-up board on the fly. That’s fine for a handful of projects. As the portfolio grows, it gets slow and unreliable, because the work scales with every board you add.

3. Deletes leave litter

Delete a project board and its portfolio row disappears with it — but not the other way around. Delete the portfolio item and the board stays behind. So the more your team actually uses the system, the more orphaned entries pile up, with no clean way to clear them. At scale, the “single source of truth” slowly fills with clutter.

4. The double-license trap

To get the portfolio solution, you bought Work Management Enterprise on top of the rest of your monday.com. When it doesn’t hold up and the reporting gets rebuilt natively, the honest question follows: then why are we paying for both? Most of the time, you don’t need to.

Executives don’t want an aggregated summary of all the weeds. They want to think about the high-level boards strategically. They don’t need it all rolling up.

The deeper reason it’s the wrong model

Every one of those failures traces back to one assumption: that leaders want every low-level task rolled up into one place. They don’t. Executives think in terms of strategic, high-level boards — not an aggregated summary of every task across every team. The fix isn’t a better rollup. It’s not rolling everything up in the first place.

What we build instead

We rebuild the same executive reporting with native boards — no special portfolio product required. The shape looks like this:

  • A high-level board where each item is a project, living as an ordinary board, connected to your master-data boards: organization, owner, referrer, the opportunity it came from.
  • Reporting from dashboards that group by that master data — open deliveries by industry, by lead, by owner — without ever scanning the underlying task boards. The insight comes from how the data is connected, not from rolling the weeds upward.
  • Permissions handled at the board level. The high-level board can be open to the whole company; individual boards can be private or shared to a specific guest scoped to just that board; sensitive columns can be restricted. Locking one board down doesn’t take the rest of the system with it.

The result scales to the limits of any normal monday.com board, behaves predictably, and doesn’t depend on a second Enterprise license to function.

Key takeaways

  • The default portfolio/project rollup demos well and breaks at scale.
  • Three failure modes: one private board breaks the whole view, front-end recalculation, and delete-asymmetry clutter.
  • Executives want strategic high-level boards — not every task rolled up.
  • Rebuild it with native boards, connected/mirror columns, and dashboards that group by master data.
  • You probably don’t need the second (Enterprise) license you bought for the portfolio feature.
