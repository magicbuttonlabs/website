Wait — can everyone in my account see this board?

By Ethan Foulkes June 2, 2026 6 min read

It usually surfaces the moment someone from IT or HR really looks at the account: “Hang on. We’ve got salaries on a board in here — review notes, someone’s social security number. Can everyone with a login just… see this?”

It’s one of the most common “why didn’t anyone tell me?!” moments we run into. Good news: the answer is genuinely reassuring. The gotcha nobody mentions is at the end — stay with me.

The reassuring part: private boards are invisible — even to admins

Make a board private and it doesn’t just tuck away — it disappears for everyone who isn’t invited to it. The moment you flip a board to private, it drops out of the admin panel entirely. It does not matter whether you’re an admin, a member, or a viewer; what governs access is the board’s own permission setting, not your account role.

So your HR board, your finance board, the unannounced project you’re not ready to share — those can be genuinely locked down. If you have not been invited to a private board, you will never see it. Not as an admin, not as the account owner, not ever.

One wrinkle: names versus contents

There’s a content directory where an admin can see that a private board exists, by name — but they can’t open it, see inside it, or add themselves to it. The practical takeaway: a private board’s contents are safe, so don’t undo that by putting something confidential in the board’s title.

Letting the right outsiders in: shareable boards

The flip side of private is shareable. Change a board to shareable and you can invite external guests — a client, a contractor — into that one board and nothing else. They see only what you invited them to: not your workspace, not your folder structure, not your other boards. It’s how you collaborate with a customer without handing over the keys to everything.

The gotcha nobody mentions: hiding a column is not securing it

Here’s the one that catches people. Hiding a column — or hiding the main table — is about clarity, not security. It reduces clutter and keeps a view clean. It is not a permission.

Anyone who can see the board can duplicate it, or duplicate the view, and unhide everything you tucked away. So if a field just looks noisy, hide it — great. But if it’s genuinely confidential — compensation, SSNs, anything you’d be in real trouble over — hiding the column is not protection. It’s one right-click away from the whole company.

If it’s sensitive, control who can reach the board. If it’s confidential, don’t put it where a hidden column is the only thing standing between it and everyone.

When you need finer control: item-level visibility

You can also limit people to seeing only the items they’re assigned to, driven by the people column — useful for a regional manager who should see only their region, or a client who should see only their own work. It leans toward all-or-nothing per role, so it takes a little thought to set up, but it’s there when board-level access isn’t granular enough.

The short version

None of this is secret knowledge. But almost nobody gets told it up front — which is exactly how confidential data ends up one “unhide columns” away from the entire org. Set your permissions deliberately, at the board level. Don’t rely on hiding.

Key takeaways

  • Private boards are invisible to everyone not invited — admins and account owners included.
  • A board’s permission setting, not your account role, decides who can see it.
  • Admins can see a private board’s name in the content directory, but not its contents — so mind the title.
  • Shareable boards let you bring external guests into one board only.
  • Hiding a column is clarity, not security — it can be duplicated and unhidden. Protect confidential data with board access or item-level visibility instead.
