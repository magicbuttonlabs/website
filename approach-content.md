# Our Approach — Page Content

> **What this is:** The single source of truth for the Our Approach copy. Both the live website page (`approach.html`) and the print 2-pager (`approach-2pager.html` → `downloads/approach-2pager.pdf`) render *from* this file. Built to the Page Content Standard in WEB-STANDARDS `project-context.md` (three-block skeleton: Shared copy · Render deltas · Section map).
>
> **What this page is:** The public face of MBL's five-phase delivery framework — **Analyze → Prototype → Iterate → Handoff → Support & Optimize** — defined by Ethan Foulkes and Brandon Huff (Fathom calls 706962675 and 706984615, 2026-06-10; revised value-first in the review call of 2026-06-11). It is the sales→delivery handoff artifact: sent with every SOW ("attached is a 2-pager that explains our delivery process"), shown on the site as a nav-level page, and used verbatim as Brandon's delivery-team training material so there is zero gap between what customers are told and what the team executes.
>
> **Page category:** Non-offering methodology page. Per the Offering Primer Standard this page gets **no web primer** — but it does get a 2-pager, because the PDF has a real job (the SOW attachment / leave-behind). This is the first page in the "methodology" category: **full page + 2-pager, no primer.** The PDF's link points at the full page (no primer to point at).
>
> **Provenance:** All copy below is drafted from the three Fathom transcripts (two framework calls 2026-06-10; review call 2026-06-11). Deliberately *excluded* from public copy: internal hour-percentage guidelines, upsell mechanics, and references to historical client situations.
>
> **Copy posture (locked in the 2026-06-11 review):**
> - **Value-first, hours minimized — not erased.** The customer purchases *decades of monday.com experience and business expertise*, delivered through the five-phase process — not a bucket of hours. Hours/budget references are "insider baseball"; exactly **two light groundings survive**: the "Built to make every hour count" section title, and "mapped to your budget" in Analyze's deliverable (the no-surprises anchor where appetite meets budget). Everything else is reframed to needs / priorities / engagement / investment.
> - **Control framing answers the rigidity worry.** "Same proven path" can read inflexible — the counter is not "we're flexible," it's *you're in control*: it's your business; you decide where our expertise and experience get pointed.
> - **Outcomes over outputs.** Page language aims at outcomes, not outputs — which is why the phase table's right column is labeled **"What you get,"** not "Output" (the page shouldn't contradict its own framing).
> - **Support & Optimize replaces both Optimize and the "Closed" section.** The customer didn't buy a static, off-the-shelf tool — monday.com keeps doing more as the business grows. The only question after Iterate is *who does the optimizing*: their team (now that they own it) or ours. That makes phase 05 the natural, warm home for what-comes-after (new teams, training, support, new builds) — and the standalone "Closed — and clear on what's next" section is **deleted** (read too cold/transactional).

## 1. Shared Copy

### Eyebrow / document identity
- **Website eyebrow:** How We Deliver
- **2-pager topbar label:** Partner Resource · Our Delivery Approach

### Hero
- **Headline:** Five phases. *Zero surprises.* (the "Zero surprises." in accent/italic treatment)
- **Lead:** When you work with us, you purchase decades of monday.com experience and business expertise — and our delivery team gives it to you through a proven five-phase process. Every engagement follows the same path, so you already know what your journey will look like. And because it's your business, you stay in control of where we focus, the whole way.
- **Hero meta (2-pager only):** At delivery kickoff, your delivery lead maps your needs across the five phases — so the plan reflects your priorities from day one.

### Hero stats (2-pager only — see Delta B)
- **5** — phases, one accountable process
- **1** — prioritized backlog you own from phase one
- **0** — surprises, start to finish

### The three principles
- **Section eyebrow (website):** Why it works
- **Section title (website):** Built to make every hour count. *(the one deliberate light hours reference — see copy posture)*
- **Your needs, mapped** — At delivery kickoff, your delivery lead maps your needs across the five phases. The plan reflects your priorities from day one — and you decide where to point our expertise. It's your business; you're in control.
- **Focus over everything** — monday.com can do almost anything — but doing everything at once accomplishes nothing well. We aim your investment at outcomes, not outputs: the highest-ROI problems first, proven before going wider.
- **Aligned at every step** — If an idea outgrows the engagement, you hear it the moment it comes up — not at the end. Need more? We talk about it at every step.

### The five phases
- **Section title:** How your journey plays out.
- **Section lead:** Every phase has a goal and a deliverable you can see — and nothing moves forward until you've confirmed it.

**01. Analyze**
- One-liner (2-pager): Align needs, priorities, and budget. The result: a prioritized backlog you own — valuable on its own.
- Description: We learn how your business runs, where it hurts, and everything monday.com could do about it — then aim our expertise at the highest-ROI problems first. We also align on how much your team wants to own versus have us deliver. This is the most important phase: expectations get set here, so there are no surprises later.
- Goal: A shared, prioritized view of your needs — and where to focus first.
- What you get: A prioritized backlog of deliverables, mapped to your budget. It has value on its own — whether we build it, your team does, or someone else does.

**02. Prototype**
- One-liner (2-pager): We heard you — here's the initial build. Confirm the direction before we invest deeper.
- Description: We reflect what we heard in a basic working build — starting with simple diagrams of how things relate, then real boards with your data — so you can confirm the direction before we invest deeper.
- Goal: Reflect your needs in a basic build that's directionally right.
- What you get: An initial build you've seen and confirmed matches your understanding and needs.

**03. Iterate**
- One-liner (2-pager): Build out the nuanced pieces — views, dashboards, refinements — as your team tests.
- Description: With the direction confirmed, we build out the nuanced pieces that get it over the line — views, dashboards, refinements — checking off backlog goals as your team tests in the system.
- Goal: Continue the build against the backlog until the focused scope is done.
- What you get: The solution, demoed and approved — with your team testing inside it.

**04. Handoff**
- One-liner (2-pager): Your team owns it. Packaged, documented, no surprises.
- Description: We formally package the solution and close the gaps that help your team run it — recordings, walkthroughs, final touches. There should be no surprises left by the time we get here.
- Goal: Your team confidently owning the solution.
- What you get: A packaged, documented system — owned by you.

**05. Support & Optimize** *(carries an "Optional" tag in both renders)*
- One-liner (2-pager): A flexible platform keeps doing more. Grow it with us — or take it from here.
- Description: You didn't buy a static, off-the-shelf tool — you bought a flexible platform that keeps doing more as your business grows. So the only question after handoff is who does the optimizing: your team, now that you own it, or ours. Stay on with support, lean into enhancements, add new teams, training, or new builds — or take it from here. Either way, we've got you covered.
- Goal: A solution that keeps growing with your business — with us or on your own.
- What you get: Support, optimization, training, and new builds when you want them. Entirely your call.

### Count-on / won't-do (2-pager page 2 — see Delta C)
- **Kicker:** How we keep you in control
- **What you can count on:**
  - Your needs **mapped to the five phases** at delivery kickoff.
  - A backlog with **standalone value** from phase one.
  - A **flag the moment** an idea outgrows the engagement — not at the end.
- **What we won't do:**
  - **Drown you in analysis theater.** As little analysis as possible — as much as we need.
  - **Quietly build past what you signed up for.** If something needs more, we talk about it when it comes up.
  - **Hand off a system your team can't run.**

### Outcomes (2-pager page 2)
1. **You always know where things stand** — Needs mapped at kickoff, confirmed phase by phase, flagged the moment anything outgrows the plan.
2. **A backlog you own from phase one** — A prioritized list of everything your system could do — valuable whether we build it or you do.
3. **A handoff with nothing left unsaid** — Demoed, approved, packaged, documented — with support and optimization on tap if you want them.

### CTA
- **Website CTA band:** Ready to supercharge your business? / Five proven phases, decades of expertise — pointed wherever your business needs them. New teams, training, builds, support: we've got you covered. / Buttons: Contact Us · Download the 2-pager
- **2-pager CTA:** kicker "See the full approach" · headline "Your business, *supercharged*" · URL **magicbuttonlabs.com/approach** · sub "The five phases · Goals and deliverables · Support & Optimize when you want it"

## 2. Render Deltas

### Delta 0 — Two artifacts, not three
- This page has **no web primer** (non-offering page; Offering Primer Standard). The set is full page + print 2-pager only. The 2-pager's CTA points at the **full page** (`magicbuttonlabs.com/approach`) — there is no primer URL to point at.
- The full page carries the **"Download the 2-pager"** action (hero + CTA band) pointing at `downloads/approach-2pager.pdf` — unlike offering pages, where the PDF is reached via the primer. Rationale: this PDF's job is the SOW attachment; the page is also where a mid-engagement customer re-finds it.

### Delta A — Goal/What-you-get depth
- **Website:** Every phase row carries the full Description plus explicit **Goal** and **What you get** cells.
- **2-pager:** Phases compress to the one-liners; the kicker line carries "each with a goal and a deliverable you confirm." Driven by the 5-across height budget.
- **Why:** The goal/deliverable structure is the heart of the framework (it doubles as Brandon's team training material) — the website has room to show it; the print page does not.

### Delta B — Hero stats
- **Website:** No stat strip (consistent with the site-wide pattern — stats are a 2-pager credibility device).
- **2-pager:** 5 / 1 / 0 stat strip in the hero.

### Delta C — Principles vs. count-on/won't-do
- **Website:** The three principles render as a 3-card grid ("Your needs, mapped" / "Focus over everything" / "Aligned at every step") near the top.
- **2-pager:** The same material splits: the kickoff-mapping principle rides in the hero meta; the rest re-renders on page 2 as the two-column **"What you can count on / What we won't do"** band (the FB "Scoped on purpose" device, reused because it's the strongest honest-scoping cue in the kit).
- **Why:** Page 1 of the print artifact has no room for both the phase grid and a principles grid; the do/don't device earns its page-2 slot better than a softer principles restatement.

### Delta D — Five-across phase grid (non-standard column count)
- The 2-pager uses a **5-column** pillar grid (the framework default is 3). This is the first 5-across build — Ethan's call-1 instinct ("good as chevrons on the one-pager"). Copy per cell is capped at ~18 words; the Optional tag on Support & Optimize is a small pill, not extra copy. Logged against the framework's parked "non-standard column counts" question.

### Vocabulary note — Foundation Build overlap *(decision: option a, 2026-06-10)*
- Foundation Build's canonical stages are **Analyze / Prototype / Align** — unchanged, still canonical for that offering. The delivery framework here is **Analyze / Prototype / Iterate / Handoff / Support & Optimize**. The shared words are intentional: Foundation Build is effectively a compressed special case of the same motion (analyze, prototype, then align/hand over a validated starting point). Both vocabularies ship; neither renames the other. Revisit only if buyer confusion shows up. Cross-noted in `foundation-build-content.md`.
