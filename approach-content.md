>
> **What this page is:** The public face of MBL's five-phase delivery framework — **Analyze → Prototype → Iterate → Optimize → Handoff** — defined by Ethan Foulkes and Brandon Huff (Fathom calls 706962675 and 706984615, 2026-06-10). It is the sales→delivery handoff artifact: sent with every SOW ("attached is a 2-pager that explains our delivery process"), shown on the site as a nav-level page, and used verbatim as Brandon's delivery-team training material so there is zero gap between what customers are told and what the team executes.

> **Page category:** Non-offering methodology page. Per the Offering Primer Standard this page gets **no web primer** — but it does get a 2-pager, because the PDF has a real job (the SOW attachment / leave-behind). > **Provenance:** All copy below is drafted from the two 2026-06-10 Fathom transcripts. ## 1. Shared Copy

### Eyebrow / document identity
- **Website eyebrow:** How We Deliver
- **2-pager topbar label:** Partner Resource · Our Delivery Approach

### Hero
- **Headline:** Five phases. *Zero surprises.* (the "Zero surprises." in accent/italic treatment)
- **Lead:** When you work with us, you purchase a bucket of hours — and our delivery team runs a proven five-phase process to turn those hours into results. Every engagement follows the same path, so you always know where your hours are going, what's coming next, and what it would take to go further.
- **Hero meta (2-pager only):** At delivery kickoff, your delivery lead maps your hours across the five phases — so the plan reflects your budget from day one.

### Hero stats (2-pager only — see Delta B)
- **5** — phases, one accountable process
- **1** — prioritized backlog you own from phase one
- **0** — surprises about where your hours went

### The three principles ("Your hours, working")
- **Section title (website):** Built to make every hour count.
- **Your hours, mapped** — At delivery kickoff, your delivery lead allocates your hours across the five phases. The plan reflects your budget from day one — not a vague bucket that runs out unannounced.
- **Focus over everything** — monday.com can do almost anything. Your hours can't do all of it at once. We aim your budget at the highest-ROI problems first — and prove them out before going wider.
- **Aligned at every step** — If something needs more hours than the engagement holds, you hear it the moment it comes up — not at the end. Need more? We talk about it at every step.

### The five phases
- **Section title:** How your hours become outcomes.
- **Section lead:** Every phase has a goal and an output you can see — and nothing moves forward until you've confirmed it.

**01. Analyze**
- One-liner (2-pager): Align needs, priorities, and budget. The output: a prioritized backlog you own — valuable on its own.
- Description: We learn how your business runs, where it hurts, and everything monday.com could do about it — then aim your hours at the highest-ROI problems first. We also align on how much your team wants to own versus have us deliver. This is the most important phase: if the appetite is bigger than the budget, you find out here — not at the end.
- Goal: A shared, prioritized view of your needs — sized honestly against your hours.
- Output: A prioritized backlog of deliverables, mapped to your budget. It has value on its own — whether we build it, your team does, or someone else does.

**02. Prototype**
- One-liner (2-pager): We heard you — here's the initial build. Confirm the direction before we invest deeper.
- Description: We reflect what we heard in a basic working build — starting with simple diagrams of how things relate, then real boards with your data — so you can confirm the direction before we invest deeper.
- Goal: Reflect your needs in a basic build that's directionally right.
- Output: An initial build you've seen and confirmed matches your understanding and needs.

**03. Iterate**
- One-liner (2-pager): Build out the nuanced pieces — views, dashboards, refinements — as your team tests.
- Description: With the direction confirmed, we build out the nuanced pieces that get it over the line — views, dashboards, refinements — checking off backlog goals as your team tests in the system.
- Goal: Continue the build against the backlog until the focused scope is done.
- Output: The solution, demoed and approved — with your team testing inside it.

**04. Optimize** *(carries an "Optional" tag in both renders)*
- One-liner (2-pager): Optional. If the core is approved and hours remain, we extend what matters most.
- Description: If the core is approved and hours remain, we lean into what extends it most — a basic integration, an advanced dashboard, more views per role. If the budget's done, we skip straight to Handoff. No padding.
- Goal: Extend an approved solution with the enhancements that matter most.
- Output: Each enhancement demoed and approved — or a deliberate decision to skip this phase.

**05. Handoff**
- One-liner (2-pager): Your team owns it. Packaged, documented, no surprises — minimal hours, by design.
- Description: We formally package the solution and close the gaps that help your team run it — recordings, walkthroughs, final touches. Minimal hours, by design. There should be no surprises left by the time we get here.
- Goal: Your team confidently owning the solution.
- Output: A packaged, documented system — owned by you.

### What comes after ("Closed")
- **Section title:** Closed — and clear on what's next.
- **Copy:** Every engagement closes completely — the work accepted, the solution owned by your team. What remains is the backlog: the prioritized list of everything else your system could do. Tackle it in-house, or bring us back — more hours, a retainer, a new team, the next phase. Either way, you know exactly what's next.

### Count-on / won't-do (2-pager page 2 — see Delta C)
- **What you can count on:**
  - Your hours **mapped to the five phases** at delivery kickoff.
  - A backlog with **standalone value** from phase one.
  - A **flag the moment** an ask outgrows the hours — not at the end.
- **What we won't do:**
  - **Burn your budget on analysis theater.** As few hours as possible — as many as we need.
  - **Quietly build past your hours.** If you need more, we talk about it when it comes up.
  - **Hand off a system your team can't run.**

### Outcomes (2-pager page 2)
1. **You always know where your hours stand** — Mapped at kickoff, tracked by phase, flagged the moment something outgrows them.
2. **A backlog you own from phase one** — A prioritized list of everything your system could do — valuable whether we build it or you do.
3. **A handoff with nothing left unsaid** — Demoed, approved, packaged, documented. Closed completely — and clear on what's next.

### CTA
- **Website CTA band:** Ready to put your hours to work? / Five phases, one accountable process — and a backlog you'll own from day one. / Buttons: Contact Us · Download the 2-pager
- **2-pager CTA:** kicker "See the full approach" · headline "Your hours, *working*" · URL **magicbuttonlabs.com/approach** · sub "The five phases · Goals and outputs · What comes after handoff"

## 2. Render Deltas

### Delta 0 — Two artifacts, not three
- This page has **no web primer** (non-offering page; Offering Primer Standard). The set is full page + print 2-pager only. The 2-pager's CTA/QR points at the **full page** (`magicbuttonlabs.com/approach`) — there is no primer URL to point at.
- The full page carries the **"Download the 2-pager"** action (hero + CTA band) pointing at `downloads/approach-2pager.pdf` — unlike offering pages, where the PDF is reached via the primer. Rationale: this PDF's job is the SOW attachment; the page is also where a mid-engagement customer re-finds it.

### Delta A — Goal/Output depth
- **Website:** Every phase row carries the full Description plus explicit **Goal** and **Output** cells.
- **2-pager:** Phases compress to the one-liners; the kicker line carries "each with a goal and an output." Driven by the 5-across height budget.
- **Why:** The Goal/Output structure is the heart of the framework (it doubles as Brandon's team training material) — the website has room to show it; the print page does not.

### Delta B — Hero stats
- **Website:** No stat strip (consistent with the site-wide pattern — stats are a 2-pager credibility device).
- **2-pager:** 5 / 1 / 0 stat strip in the hero.

### Delta C — Principles vs. count-on/won't-do
- **Website:** The three principles render as a 3-card grid ("Your hours, mapped" / "Focus over everything" / "Aligned at every step") near the top.
- **2-pager:** The same material splits: the kickoff-mapping principle rides in the hero meta; the rest re-renders on page 2 as the two-column **"What you can count on / What we won't do"** band (the FB "Scoped on purpose" device, reused because it's the strongest honest-scoping cue in the kit).
- **Why:** Page 1 of the print artifact has no room for both the phase grid and a principles grid; the do/don't device earns its page-2 slot better than a softer principles restatement.

### Delta D — Five-across phase grid (non-standard column count)
- The 2-pager uses a **5-column** pillar grid (the framework default is 3). This is the first 5-across build — Ethan's call-1 instinct ("good as chevrons on the one-pager"). Copy per cell is capped at ~18 words; the Optional tag on Optimize is a small pill, not extra copy. Logged against the framework's parked "non-standard column counts" question.

### Vocabulary note — Foundation Build overlap *(decision: option a, 2026-06-10)*
- Foundation Build's canonical stages are **Analyze / Prototype / Align** — unchanged, still canonical for that offering. The delivery framework here is **Analyze / Prototype / Iterate / Optimize / Handoff**. The shared words are intentional: Foundation Build is effectively a compressed special case of the same motion (analyze, prototype, then align/hand over a validated starting point). Both vocabularies ship; neither renames the other. Revisit only if buyer confusion shows up. Cross-noted in `foundation-build-content.md`.
