Every company switching to monday.com is afraid of the same thing

By Brandon Huff June 2, 2026 5 min read

“I know we need to do this. But I’m nervous about a big change — and honestly, it’s hard enough to get my team into a shared doc. Just tell me it’s going to be okay.”

We hear that on almost every migration call. The tool they’re leaving changes — HubSpot, Salesforce, Asana, Wrike, Basecamp, a tangle of spreadsheets — but the fear is nearly word for word the same. Different company, same call.

The good news: it’s almost always aimed at the wrong thing.

You’re not imagining it — the fear is universal

If you’re dreading the switch, you’re in good company. Almost everyone moving to monday.com walks in carrying two versions of the same worry: that the migration itself will be a massive, error-prone slog, and that even if the data makes it across, the team won’t actually use the new thing. Both are reasonable. One of them is mostly misplaced.

But it’s usually the wrong fear

Here’s what we’ve learned watching this play out again and again. When people picture a migration, they picture dragging years of configuration across — every workflow, automation, custom field, and quirk rebuilt one-to-one. That’s where they imagine the pain lives.

But step back. You’re leaving your old tool because you didn’t like it. And most of the time, you didn’t like it because it was set up badly in the first place — bolted together over years by people solving yesterday’s problem. So the last thing you actually want is to faithfully recreate that mess in a shiny new home.

Do you want to pay us to drag a hot mess of configurations across — or start on a clean architecture, where the migration is just the data?

What actually has to move

Once you stop trying to migrate the mess, the scary part shrinks fast. What needs to come across is the data that matters — your clients, your contacts, your active deals, your open work — not ten years of granular history you’ll never open again. We’ve had teams realize, mid-call, that they don’t need most of what they were bracing to move.

That transactional data lands on a clean, deliberately designed structure, and the move itself becomes almost boring. Often you can even do it yourself: highlight the items, move them to the right board, done. We coach you through it, or we do it for you — but it’s rarely the monster people show up afraid of.

The other half of the fear

The second worry — “I can barely get them into a shared doc” — is real, and it’s a genuinely different problem than the migration. The answer isn’t to force the tool on people. It’s to build it around how they already work, so that using it is the path of least resistance instead of one more thing. That’s its own conversation — but the fear is addressable, and it’s not a reason to stay stuck where you are.

So — is everything going to be okay?

Honestly, yes. Not because migrations are trivial, but because the part you’re most afraid of usually isn’t the part that matters. Leave the mess behind. Bring what counts. Start clean.

Key takeaways

  • The migration fear is nearly universal — and usually aimed at the wrong thing.
  • The pain people imagine is migrating configurations; you don’t want to rebuild a setup you disliked.
  • What actually moves is the data that matters — not years of history you’ll never use.
  • On a clean architecture, the move is often simple enough to do yourself.
  • Adoption is a separate, solvable worry: build around how the team already works.
Written by Brandon Huff, head of sales, Magic Button Labs — from the conversations we have with teams about to make the jump.
